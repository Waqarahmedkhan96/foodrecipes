The coal smoke trick

The dhuanr method that gives home-cooked tikka and daal the taste of a roadside dhaba, with one piece of charcoal.

That smoky flavour you taste in dhaba-style tikka and daal doesn't need a tandoor. It comes from one piece of glowing charcoal and three minutes under a lid. In Urdu this is called dhuanr.

What you need

A single lump of natural charcoal, a small steel bowl or a piece of foil folded into a cup, a teaspoon of ghee or oil and a pot with a tight lid.

How to do it

Hold the charcoal over a gas flame with tongs until it glows red all over, about four minutes. Place the steel bowl in the centre of the finished dish, set the coal inside and pour the ghee over it. As soon as it starts to smoke, cover the pot tightly and leave it for two to three minutes. Lift the lid, remove the bowl and serve.

Fatma's tipDo this with the window open and the extractor on, and never leave glowing charcoal unattended. Let it cool completely in water before throwing it away.

Where it works best

Chicken tikka, seekh kebab mince, daal masoor and chana daal all take smoke beautifully. Delicate dishes like kheer or yakhni pulao are better left without it.
