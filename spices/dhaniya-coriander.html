Spice guide · Coriandrum sativum

Dhaniya (coriander)

Dhaniya lives two lives in a Pakistani kitchen: the round, citrusy seeds ground into almost every masala, and the fresh green leaves scattered over the finished dish.

Urdu nameDhaniya
EnglishCoriander
Botanical nameCoriandrum sativum
Try it inLahori chicken karahi, Mint, coriander and tomato chutney, Yakhni chicken pulao

What is dhaniya?

Dhaniya comes from Coriandrum sativum, a plant in the same family as carrots and parsley. The dried seeds (sabut dhaniya) are warm and slightly lemony. The fresh leaves (hara dhaniya, also called cilantro) are bright and grassy. Ground seeds are called pisa dhaniya.

An ancient herb

Coriander is native to the Middle East and Mediterranean. Seeds were found in the tomb of the Egyptian king Ramesses II, it is mentioned in the Ebers Papyrus from about 1550 BC, and it was grown in Persia around 3,000 years ago. The Romans carried it across Europe, while traders brought it east to the subcontinent long ago.

Why we use it in Pakistani cooking

Ground dhaniya thickens and rounds out curries, balancing chilli heat. Coarsely crushed seeds give Lahori karahi its crunch. Whole seeds go into the potli for yakhni and into achar. Fresh hara dhaniya finishes nearly every salan, biryani and chaat, and is blended into green chutney.

How to use it well

Toast whole seeds in a dry pan until fragrant, then crush for the freshest flavour. Add ground dhaniya with other powdered spices and cook it in oil. Store fresh leaves wrapped in a damp paper towel in the fridge, or stand the stems in water like flowers.

What science says about coriander

Coriander has a long traditional use for indigestion and loss of appetite, and Germany's Commission E approved the seed for these complaints. Other claimed benefits, such as lowering blood sugar or cholesterol, come mainly from traditional use and early laboratory research, and haven't been confirmed in good human trials yet.

Safety

Coriander is safe in food amounts. Some people are allergic to it. A few people find that fresh coriander leaves taste like soap, which is linked to their genes, not to the herb being off.

Frequently asked questions

Are coriander and cilantro the same plant?

Yes. Cilantro usually means the fresh leaves and coriander the dried seeds, but both come from Coriandrum sativum.

Why does my karahi use crushed coriander seeds?

Coarsely crushed seeds give bursts of citrusy flavour and texture that ground powder can't.

Can I freeze fresh dhaniya?

Yes. Chop it, pack into an ice-cube tray with a little water or oil, and freeze. Use it in cooking rather than as a garnish.
