Spice guide · Elettaria cardamomum

Elaichi (green cardamom)

Open a jar of elaichi and the whole kitchen smells of celebrations: doodh patti chai, kheer, sheer khurma and a pot of biryani on dum.

Urdu nameElaichi
EnglishGreen cardamom
Botanical nameElettaria cardamomum
Try it inRice kheer, Sheer khurma, Shahi zarda

What is elaichi?

Green elaichi is the seed pod of Elettaria cardamomum, a plant in the ginger family. Inside each papery pod are small black seeds full of fragrant oils. Its botanical name comes from the Tamil word elettari. Badi elaichi (black cardamom) is a different, larger, smoky pod from a related plant.

The queen of spices

Cardamom is native to southern India and is now grown mainly in India and Guatemala. It was prized in the ancient world: Greeks and Romans used it in perfumes and imported it from India, and a Babylonian king reportedly grew it in his garden around the 8th century BC. Its high price is why it is often called the queen of spices, next to pepper the king.

Why we use it in Pakistani cooking

Whole pods go into the oil for biryani, pulao and qorma, and into the pan for doodh patti chai. Ground seeds perfume kheer, sheer khurma, zarda, gulab jamun syrup and mithai. A pod chewed after a meal freshens the breath.

How to use it well

Lightly crush pods before adding them so the seeds release their oils. For desserts, grind the seeds and discard the husks. Buy whole green pods; ground cardamom fades fast.

What science says about cardamom

Cardamom has a long traditional use for digestion and breath. Modern research on its health effects is still early and mostly small studies, so there aren't confirmed health benefits yet.

Safety

Cardamom is safe in normal food amounts. As with any spice, check with a doctor before taking concentrated supplements.

Frequently asked questions

What's the difference between green and black elaichi?

Green elaichi is sweet and floral for chai and desserts. Black (badi) elaichi is large and smoky, used in biryani, pulao and garam masala.

Why is cardamom so expensive?

The pods are picked by hand as they ripen, and the plant grows only in particular tropical conditions.

Can I use ground cardamom instead of pods?

Yes. About ¼ teaspoon ground equals 3 to 4 pods, but the flavour is weaker.
