Spice guide · Curcuma longa

Haldi (turmeric)

Haldi is the golden thread of Pakistani cooking. A pinch goes into almost every salan, daal and yakhni, giving colour, an earthy warmth and a scent that means home.

Urdu nameHaldi
EnglishTurmeric
Botanical nameCurcuma longa
Try it inDaal masoor, Karachi chicken biryani, Karachi beef haleem

What is haldi?

Haldi is the dried and ground rhizome (underground stem) of Curcuma longa, a plant in the ginger family. The fresh root looks like ginger with bright orange flesh. Its colour comes from curcumin, part of a group of natural compounds called curcuminoids, which are also the focus of most research into turmeric.

A spice older than history books

Turmeric has been part of South Asian cooking for thousands of years. Archaeologists from Washington State University studied cooking residues from the Indus Valley sites of Farmana and Harappa and found evidence that curries made with turmeric, ginger and garlic were eaten in the Indus civilisation, the same land where Pakistan stands today. It also has a long place in Ayurvedic, Unani, Chinese and Thai traditional medicine, where it was used for indigestion, joint pain and skin care.

Why we use it in Pakistani cooking

Haldi goes in early, usually with the onions or tomatoes during bhunai, because its raw taste needs a minute in hot oil to mellow. It gives daal its sunny yellow, colours biryani masala and yakhni, and is the base of many marinades. It is also part of shaadi traditions: the mayun or ubtan ceremony uses a haldi paste on the bride's skin before the wedding.

How to use it well

Use a light hand: ¼ to ½ teaspoon is enough for a family-sized pot, and too much tastes bitter and medicinal. Always cook it in oil for at least 30 seconds. Store ground haldi in an airtight jar away from light, and replace it once the colour fades. Haldi stains, so wipe counters and wooden spoons quickly.

What science says about turmeric

The US National Center for Complementary and Integrative Health (NCCIH) says there isn't yet enough evidence to say turmeric or curcumin is definitely beneficial for any health condition. The most promising early research is for knee osteoarthritis pain, and some studies suggest possible benefits for fatty liver and for mouth soreness during cancer treatment, but higher-quality studies are needed. Curcumin is poorly absorbed on its own. A small 1998 study found that taking it with piperine, the compound in black pepper, increased absorption by about 2,000% in volunteers, which is one reason haldi and kali mirch so often meet in the same pot.

Safety

Turmeric in food amounts is considered safe for most people. Supplements are different: NCCIH reports cases of liver injury with highly absorbable curcumin products, and they may not be safe during pregnancy. If you take medicines or plan to use turmeric supplements, speak to your doctor first.

Frequently asked questions

Is haldi the same as turmeric?

Yes. Haldi is the Urdu and Hindi name for turmeric, the ground root of the Curcuma longa plant.

Why is black pepper added with haldi?

Piperine in black pepper helps the body absorb curcumin. One study found absorption rose by about 2,000% when the two were taken together.

How much haldi should I use in a curry?

About ¼ to ½ teaspoon for a curry that serves four. More can make the dish taste bitter.
